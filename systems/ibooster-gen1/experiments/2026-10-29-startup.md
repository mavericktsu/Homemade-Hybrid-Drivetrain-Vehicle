# October 29 — Getting a reliable startup before asking for motion

## The problem

I had wired the connector and could see CAN messages in Cangaroo, but my first helper stopped with “private feedback absent for 500 ms.” I needed to establish a stable baseline before treating a failed movement as a command-protocol problem.

## What I tried

I moved the adapter to the private/Yaw bus, **H18 / L10**, at 500 kbit/s. The helper initially sent only disabled-request handshake traffic. I also checked how I was applying ignition: I had been touching the ignition lead to wake the booster, then removing it while the permanent feeds remained connected.

The unit continued operating for a while after that brief ignition connection, which made this easy to miss. I changed to maintained ignition voltage during the run. The power branches shared the same supply and ground.

## What I found

In the maintained-ignition capture, I had **11,524 valid feedback frames by about 76.8 seconds**, zero reported CAN error frames, and a candidate-mode transition from 4 to 7. Rest position was 320 raw. That gave me a much better starting point than the earlier run with ACK errors and missing feedback.

I then tried one tiny **+8 raw-count step**, from 320 to 328, limited to 0.5 seconds. I didn't see or hear convincing movement. The log only showed a very small change around 321–322. I manually pressed the rod later to confirm the assembly responded; that larger recorded motion wasn't the remote command working.

## What I learned

Seeing feedback doesn't establish that the wake inputs are right, and “the test ran” doesn't establish that the requested motion happened. I needed maintained ignition, a stable disabled-request baseline, and a measured movement check.

The saved sources are `handshake_20261029_155201_060791.csv` and `small_step_20261029_160146_801189.csv`, retained in my working records. Later tests demonstrated much clearer commanded movement and established a [travel scale](2026-10-30-travel-and-gain.md).
